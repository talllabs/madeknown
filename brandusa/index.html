B2B Destination + Human Story   Tourism Papua New Guinea

Two Stories, One Assignment, New York

Tourism Papua New Guinea brought us to New York for a B2B media lunch — a polished, press-facing event designed to put the destination in front of travel media and industry decision-makers. We delivered that: a sharp, professional event capture that served the tourism authority's trade marketing goals.

But while we were there, we noticed something. Fabian Paino — a Papua New Guinean visiting New York for the very first time — was experiencing America with completely fresh eyes. We pitched the client on a second piece: a short, documentary-style portrait of Fabian navigating the city, discovering it the way a first-time traveler discovers any place.

They said yes. The result was two distinct pieces from one production: a B2B event film and an authentic human travel story. We think that's what a genuine creative partnership looks like.

WHY WE'RE SHARING:  It demonstrates both B2B event content and human-centered destination storytelling — and our instinct to find the deeper story inside the assignment.

Lifestyle Commercial + Mini-Documentary   Lutheran World Relief

Coffee Is Life  ·  Nicaragua

Coffee Is Life is a lifestyle commercial centered on the ritual of the morning cup — warm, cinematic, and rooted in everyday feeling. But instead of actors and scripts, we went to Nicaragua and sat with the farming families whose hands grow the coffee. We took their actual words, their real expressions, and built the commercial around them. Non-actors. Real stories. Scripted from life.

Why We're Sharing:  It demonstrates tonal range across formats, the ability to build content around real people and real words, and the craft to make both feel genuinely cinematic.
Documentary / Social Impact   Andean Health & Development

Andes in Motion

Andean Health & Development wanted to connect with donors — but they wanted something that would move people. Something that captured what the work actually felt like from the inside.

We went to the Andes. We followed the people doing the work and the people whose lives it touched. We filmed against landscapes that were cinematic by nature and intimate by choice. The brief was emotional engagement — and we treated it like a short documentary, not a fundraising appeal.

The result is a film that shows what compassionate, sustained aid looks like when it's woven into a community — told from the inside out.

Why We're Sharing:  It shows our ability to find and tell stories that are emotionally resonant, visually cinematic, and rooted in real place — the foundation of great destination content.

Mini-Documentary   Lutheran World Relief

Mini Doc — Daisy, Peru

One of our earliest pieces. It has stood the test of time.

Daisy lives in Peru. Her landscape is extraordinary. Her life is full. The film is simple, slow, and powerful — a quiet portrait of one person in one place, told with patience and care.

No voiceover. No title cards explaining the significance of what you're seeing. Just Daisy, her world, and the camera giving it the space it deserves.

We include it because it represents something we believe about storytelling: that when you slow down, stay close, and let people be themselves on screen, something real comes through. That quality doesn't age.

Relevant to Brand USA because the best destination content works the same way — human-centered, unhurried, and rooted in a specific place and person. This film is proof that we knew how to do that from the beginning.
